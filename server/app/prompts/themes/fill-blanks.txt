## Step 2: Design — Fill in the blanks

Build a fill-in-the-blanks experience: the student first reads the material in short pieces, then recalls the key terms by filling gaps in the same sentences. Follow the structure below exactly; adapt only what the "Adapt to this student" section allows. Button labels and screen text in quotes are examples; write them in the material's language.

### Why it works

- Retrieval practice: recalling information strengthens memory more than reading it again (Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Adesope et al. 2017, meta-analysis of over 100 studies).
- Immediate, specific feedback helps students correct mistakes before they stick (Hattie & Timperley 2007).
- Short pieces with one idea each lower the load on working memory (segmenting principle, Mayer).
- A word bank turns recall into recognition and removes the spelling burden, which matters for students with dyslexia.

Use these in the "Why it looks like this" box, choosing the ones that match this student.

### Who it fits

Students preparing for a test, students who need to memorise terms, and students who say they forget what they read. Works for ADHD (short active steps) and dyslexia (with the word bank).

### Screens, in this order

1. Start screen: the topic title, one sentence on what they will do ("First read, then fill in the missing words"), the number of rounds, and a Start button.
2. For each round (4 to 6 rounds in total, one idea per round; if the material has more ideas, group related ones so every key fact is still covered):
   a. Read screen: 2 to 4 short sentences about one idea, with the key terms in bold. A "I'm ready" button.
   b. Blanks screen: the same sentences, with the bold key terms replaced by blanks. The read text is no longer visible. A "Check" button, and a "Peek at the text" button that shows the read screen again without penalty.
   c. Feedback appears on the same blanks screen after Check (see below). Then a Next button.
3. Final review screen: one mixed round of 5 to 8 blanks taken from earlier rounds, in new sentences or a different order, so it isn't just a copy. Use the same input mode as the rounds.
4. Done screen: how many blanks they got right on the first try, a short encouraging message, the list of all key terms with a one-line meaning each, and a "Try again" button that restarts from round 1.

Always show progress at the top, such as "Round 2 of 5".

### Choosing the blanks

- Blank only key terms: subject words, names of processes, parts, people, dates, numbers or formulas that the material is about.
- Never blank articles, pronouns, linking words, or words the sentence gives away.
- 1 to 2 blanks per sentence, never two blanks next to each other, 2 to 5 blanks per round, 12 to 25 blanks across all rounds, plus the final review.
- Each blank must have one clear right answer from the context. If a term has accepted alternatives (a synonym, a plural, a common abbreviation), accept all of them.
- Every key idea of the material must appear in at least one blank.

### Answer input

- Word bank mode: show the answers for the current round as buttons above the sentences, plus 1 to 2 plausible wrong options from the same material. The student picks a blank, then a word (or the other way round). Used words are marked as used but can be moved back. Must work fully by keyboard.
- Typing mode: a text field inside each blank, sized to fit about the answer's length, with no autocomplete from the browser.
- Use word bank mode if the student has dyslexia, struggles with spelling, is younger, or feels unsure. Otherwise use typing mode, with a visible toggle to switch to the word bank at any time.

### Checking answers

- Ignore upper and lower case, extra spaces, and punctuation at the ends.
- In typing mode, accept small typos: none for words of up to 4 letters, up to 1 wrong letter for 5 to 8 letters, up to 2 for longer words. Never accept a typo that turns the answer into another key term or word bank option (e.g. "cation" for "anion"). Show the correct spelling next to an accepted answer with a typo, without marking it wrong.
- Numbers, symbols, and abbreviations must be exact.

### Feedback and hints

- After Check, mark each blank as right or not yet, using an icon and text as well as color.
- Each wrong blank gets one short neutral line: the correct answer and why, in one sentence ("It's mitochondria: they make the cell's energy").
- Before checking, each blank can show a hint on request: first the first letter, then the number of letters. Using a hint is fine and is not counted against them.
- Allow one retry of the wrong blanks before showing the answers. Then reveal the answers and let them continue.

### Adapt to this student

- Word bank or typing, as above.
- Round count and blanks per round, within the ranges above: fewer and shorter for attention difficulties.
- Example sentences and wrong options in the word bank can use the student's interests, as long as the key facts stay correct.
- Offer read-aloud (speechSynthesis) on read screens and for the word bank if they have reading difficulties.

### Always

- Next and Back buttons; nothing moves on by itself, and there is no timer.
- One round on screen at a time.
- Score shows only as encouragement at the end, never as a running penalty.

### Don't

- Don't show the answers on the blanks screen before the student checks or asks.
- Don't blank more than half of the words in a sentence, or make a sentence that can't be understood with its blanks.
- Don't use dropdowns inside sentences or drag-and-drop that only works with a mouse.
- Don't add extra games, timers, points, streaks, or leaderboards.
- Don't change the facts between the read screen and the blanks screen.
- Don't mark an answer wrong because of case, spacing, or an accepted synonym.
