## Step 1: Research

Find learning strategies with good evidence for this student's profile (e.g. chunking and reduced visual load for ADHD; spacing, readable fonts and text-to-speech for dyslexia; retrieval practice, immediate feedback and interest-based examples for motivation). Prefer research reviews, university or clinical sources, and accessibility guidelines such as WCAG and the British Dyslexia Association style guide. Pick the 1 to 3 strategies that best fit this student, giving extra weight to what they say already works.

## Step 2: Design

Choose one main way to work through the material, plus optional helpers. Ideas, not a fixed list: short chunks with one idea per screen and visible progress; a quiz or flashcards with instant, kind feedback; fill in the blanks; a focus mode that hides everything except the current step; read-aloud with word highlighting (browser speechSynthesis); one word at a time at an adjustable speed; bionic text with a toggle; analogies built around the student's interests; a small interactive simulation of a process.

Keep it simple. One focused experience that works beats many features that confuse. A session should take about 10 minutes and end with a clear "done" moment.

## Step 3: Build

Write ONE complete, self-contained HTML document.

Hard technical rules, because it runs in a locked-down sandboxed iframe:
- All CSS and JavaScript inline. No external scripts, stylesheets, fonts, images, CDNs, or fetch/XHR/WebSocket calls; they are blocked and the page will break.
- Images only as inline SVG, canvas, or data: URIs.
- No localStorage, sessionStorage, cookies, or IndexedDB (they throw in the sandbox). Keep state in JavaScript variables.
- No alert(), confirm(), prompt(), window.open(), or form submissions. Show messages inside the page.
- Must work offline in current Chrome, Edge, Safari, and Firefox. No build step, no frameworks.
- Keep it under about 200 KB.

Accessibility rules:
- Calm, uncluttered layout. One main thing on screen at a time. No flashing; any animation is short and can be turned off.
- Readable text: sans-serif system font stack, at least 18px, line height 1.5 or more, left-aligned, lines no longer than about 70 characters, no italics or all caps for body text.
- High contrast on a soft off-white background. Don't rely on color alone to show right or wrong.
- Large click targets, full keyboard support, visible focus, semantic HTML, and ARIA labels. Announce feedback with aria-live.
- Plain, friendly, encouraging language with short sentences. Mistakes get a neutral correction and the reason, never a penalty.
- The student controls the pace: next and back buttons, nothing auto-advances.

Content rules:
- Cover every key idea of the material or topic. Don't drop important facts.
- Don't invent facts. Analogies and interest-based examples are fine if they're clearly examples.
- Write all text the student sees in the same language as the material.

At the top of the page, add a small collapsible section titled "Why it looks like this" (translated into the student's language) that explains in 2 to 4 short sentences, addressed to the student, which strategies you chose and why, with short source names.
